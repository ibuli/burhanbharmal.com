Ever noticed how software architects tend to fall into one of two camps?

The first writes endless documents nobody reads.

The second reviews every PR, joins every design discussion, and proudly becomes the team's biggest bottleneck. 

I've come to believe the real job isn't to control the codebase.

It's to build systems that make good engineering the default outcome.

That means:

• Creating a culture of ownership

• Building strong feedback loops with tests, typing, and observability

• Automating everything that leads to repetitive debates and manual reviews 

One line that made me laugh:

"Build software such that slop monkeys can do their thing safely." 

Funny, but also a surprisingly good definition of architecture.

The goal isn't to be the smartest person in every room. It's to create an environment where teams can move fast without constantly stepping on rakes.

Because at scale, architecture is less about code and more about people.

Garden the system, not the codebase

You have to garden the system, not the codebase. It's socio-technical, and humans are 3/4ths of the challenge. Computers are easy.

Loop engineering, the kids are calling it.
