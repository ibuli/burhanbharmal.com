## The problem

Useful links, articles, and ideas are easy to save and difficult to return to. A bookmark manager, notes app, and feed reader can each solve part of the problem while leaving the overall workflow fragmented.

I built Arkive around a single place to collect, organize, and revisit that material.

## My role

Arkive is an independent product. I work on its product experience and implementation, using React, TypeScript, and Cloudflare. The original screenshots on this page show the home screen, note-taking experience, and feed reader.

## What I built

- A bookmark workflow for saving and organizing links.
- Notes for capturing and developing ideas.
- An RSS reader for following sources in the same space.
- Search and organization across the product’s everyday workflows.

## Product and engineering considerations

### One product, several ways of working

Reading feeds, organizing bookmarks, and writing notes are different tasks. Arkive brings them into one product while giving each workflow a focused interface. The gallery shows how the different views carry that idea.

### Local-first ownership

A local-first product puts the user’s own data at the center of the experience. It also makes synchronization, recovery, and consistency important engineering concerns. Those are useful questions to settle early in any product with local data and multiple devices.

### Iteration around actual use

The product includes a roadmap for feature requests and a route for general feedback. That creates a way to develop the product around what people need from it.

## Delivered outcome

Arkive brings bookmarks, notes, and feeds into a working product.

[Explore Arkive](https://arkive.me)

## What this demonstrates

Taking an independent product from a recurring problem to a coherent interface, with several connected workflows. It is relevant to teams building a new product or bringing more consistency to an existing one.
