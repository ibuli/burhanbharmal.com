Chances are, you've already used a basic AI agent without even realising it. If you've ever asked a smart speaker to play a song or used a GPS to navigate through traffic, you've interacted with one.

The key difference between an AI agent and a standard program is autonomy. A regular program is rigid, it just follows a script. An agent, on the other hand, observes what’s happening around it (digitally or physically), processes that information, and then figures out the best way to complete its assigned task. 

It’s a lot like delegating to a capable personal assistant. You wouldn't tell them exactly how to book a flight listing every single click and form field. You just give them the goal: "Find me the cheapest flight to New York next Tuesday." That assistant then uses their own knowledge and tools to get it done. AI agents do the very same thing, just in the digital world. 

This is why they're attracting so much attention and investment. The global market for AI agents is booming, projected to hit $7.63 billion by 2025, up from $5.4 billion in 2022. North America currently holds the biggest slice of the pie with about 40% of the market share, but the Asia-Pacific region is the one to watch, with its growth turbocharged by rapid digitalization.
